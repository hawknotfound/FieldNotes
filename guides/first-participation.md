# How to Prepare for Your First Event

A practical guide for students attending a hackathon, competition, or conference for the first time.

---

## Before the event (2-4 weeks out)

### 1. Read everything about the event

- Event website, schedule, rules, FAQ
- Check the format: online or offline? Individual or team?
- What to submit: code, presentation, report?
- Judging criteria: what matters most?

### 2. Register properly

- Complete registration well before the deadline
- If team registration, make sure all members register
- Join the event communication channel (Slack, Discord, Telegram)
- Read all emails from organizers

### 3. Prepare your skills

For a hackathon:
- Practice with the tech stack you plan to use
- Prepare templates or boilerplate code
- Test APIs you might need
- Prepare a simple project from scratch to warm up

For a competition:
- Solve previous year papers
- Time yourself to understand pacing
- Identify your weak areas and focus on them

For a conference:
- Research speakers and sessions
- Prepare questions you want to ask
- Plan which sessions to attend

### 4. Logistics

If traveling:
- Book travel early (trains fill up)
- Book accommodation (or check if the event provides it)
- Pack essentials (see [first-hackathon-guide.md](../hackathons/first-hackathon-guide.md))
- Download offline maps of the city
- Save emergency numbers

---

## During the event

### 5. Day 1

- Arrive early/on time
- Orient yourself: venue layout, washrooms, food area
- Attend the opening ceremony or briefing
- Meet your team (if new) or other participants
- Start working immediately — do not waste time

### 6. During the event

- Stick to your plan but adapt when needed
- Ask for help (mentors, organizers, other participants)
- Take care of your health: eat, drink water, sleep
- Network during breaks — talk to other participants
- Document your work for the final presentation

### 7. The final presentation

- Practice at least twice
- Time yourself
- Have a backup demo (screenshots, video)
- Speak clearly, not too fast
- Answer questions honestly — if you do not know, say so

---

## After the event

### 8. Debrief

- What went well? What would you do differently?
- Upload your project to GitHub with a good README
- Write about your experience (LinkedIn post, blog)
- Connect with people you met

### 9. Follow up

- Connect with judges, mentors, and organizers on LinkedIn
- Send thank-you messages to mentors who helped you
- If you won or placed, update your resume and profiles
- Share what you learned with juniors or classmates

---

## Common mistakes by first-timers

| Mistake | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Trying to build something too complex | Build something simple that works |
| Working alone all the time | Collaborate, ask questions |
| Not practicing the pitch | Rehearse 3-4 times |
| Ignoring food and sleep | Take care of basic needs |
| Starting at the last minute | Begin early, iterate fast |
| Not reading the rules | Read everything twice |

---

## Mindset

- You are not expected to win your first event
- The goal is to learn how events work
- Every participation makes the next one easier
- Experience > winning (initially)
- Incomplete submissions teach more than not participating at all
