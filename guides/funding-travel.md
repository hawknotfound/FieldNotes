# How to Fund Your Event Travel

Practical ways for Indian students to afford travel for hackathons, competitions, and conferences.

---

## Before you spend your own money

### Use student discounts

| Transport | Discount | How to get |
|---|---|---|
| Indian Railways | 40-50% on Sleeper/3AC | Student concession form from college |
| Intercity buses | 10-20% | Student ID card, some bus aggregators |
| Flights | 5-10% | Student fare on Skyscanner, ClearTrip |
| Metro | Student passes | Apply at metro stations |

Indian Railways offers concession for students traveling for academic purposes including competitions. Get a Student Concession Certificate from your college.

---

### Check if the event covers costs

Many hackathons and competitions provide:
- **Food** during the event (most do)
- **Accommodation** for finalists (SIH, many corporate hackathons)
- **Travel reimbursement** (some national-level events)
- **Swag kits** (t-shirts, bags — saves packing)

Always check the FAQ or contact organizers about participant support.

---

### Ask your college

Your college may have funding for:
- **Innovation cell budget** — if you are representing the college
- **Seed funding** for competition participation
- **Conference/travel grants** — some colleges have specific funds
- **Sponsorship** — your department head or Dean might approve

Prepare a one-page proposal with:
- Event details and why it matters
- How many students are going
- Total estimated cost
- What the college gets (visibility, branding, recognition)

## Affordable transport strategies

### Travel by train

- Book early (tatkal is expensive)
- Use student concession
- Travel overnight to save on accommodation
- General class is very cheap but uncomfortable for long distances

### Travel by bus

- Private buses on platforms like RedBus, IntrCity
- Overnight sleeper buses save a night's accommodation
- Book 2-3 weeks in advance for lowest fares

### Travel in groups

- Splitting a cab or auto-rickshaw from station to venue
- Group train bookings sometimes get slight discounts
- Shared accommodation reduces per-person cost

## Budget accommodation

| Type | Cost/night | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Hostels (Zostel, etc.) | ₹500-1000 | Solo or pair travelers |
| PG dorms | ₹300-600 | Groups |
| Budget hotels (OYO) | ₹600-1200 | 2-4 people (split) |
| College guest houses | Free-500 | If your college has a chapter there |
| Event-provided stay | Free | Best option — check first |
| Dharamshalas | ₹200-400 | Very budget, basic |

---

## Other ways to save

- **Cook some meals** — stay at a place with a kitchen
- **Pack snacks** — avoid expensive convenience store food
- **Use student IDs** — many places give student discounts
- **Stay with friends** — if you know someone in the city
- **Travel light** — avoiding baggage fees on buses/trains

---

## Earning before the event

- **Freelance** — small coding/writing/design projects
- **Online tutoring** — teach what you know
- **Campus freelancing** — help classmates with projects
- **Part-time gigs** — food delivery, retail (during breaks)
- **Sell old items** — declutter and earn

## Sample budget breakdown

For a 3-day hackathon in Bangalore from Delhi:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Train (Sleeper, student concession) | ₹800 |
| Accommodation (hostel, 2 nights) | ₹1,200 |
| Food (₹300/day × 3) | ₹900 |
| Local transport | ₹300 |
| Miscellaneous | ₹200 |
| **Total** | **₹3,400** |

With team of 4 sharing a room, per-person cost drops to ₹2,600.

---

## Important notes

- Always keep ₹500-1000 emergency cash
- Carry a water bottle and reusable container
- Some events reimburse travel for selected participants — check in advance
- Start saving 2-3 months before the event
- If the event is too expensive, look for a similar event closer to your city
