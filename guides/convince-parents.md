# How to Convince Your Parents to Let You Attend Events

A practical guide for Indian students who need parental approval to travel for hackathons, competitions, or conferences.

---

## Understand their concerns

Most Indian parents worry about:
- **Safety** — traveling alone, staying in unknown cities
- **Academics** — time away from studies, missing classes
- **Money** — cost of travel, accommodation, food
- **Legitimacy** — is this a real opportunity or a waste of time
- **Unknown environment** — they do not know what happens at these events

Your job is to address each concern with specific information.

---

## Step-by-step strategy

### 1. Find the right event first

Before talking to your parents, be prepared with:
- Exact name and dates of the event
- Website and official communication
- Organizer details (government, college, company)
- Schedule and agenda
- Registration fee (if any)

### 2. Explain what the event is

Use specific, concrete terms:
- "It is a 24-hour competition where students build software solutions."
- "Smart India Hackathon is organized by the Ministry of Education."
- "Participants get certificates from the government."

Avoid vague descriptions like "it is a tech event" or "it will be fun."

### 3. Address safety specifically

Indian parents need to know:
- Your travel route (train/flight number, timings)
- Where you will stay (hostel/hotel name, address)
- Who you are going with (names, numbers of teammates)
- Emergency contact numbers (event organizers, local contact)
- That the event venue is a secure campus

**Offer to share live location during the trip.**

### 4. Address academic concerns

- Check if the event overlaps with exams. If it does, choose another.
- Get permission from a teacher or professor beforehand.
- Explain that the skills you learn (teamwork, problem-solving, projects) are not taught in class.
- Some colleges even count hackathon participation as extra credit — find out.

### 5. Address cost concerns

Prepare a clear budget:
```
Travel: ₹XXX (train/bus)
Accommodation: ₹XXX per night
Food: ₹XXX per day
Registration: free/₹XXX
Total: ₹XXX
```

If the event provides food and accommodation (many do), highlight that.
Offer to contribute from your own savings or earn through freelancing.

### 6. Show legitimacy

Share:
- Screenshots of the official event website
- Previous year photos or news articles
- LinkedIn profiles of past participants
- Organizer's official communication

Parents trust government events more than private ones. Leverage that when applicable.

### 7. Build a track record

Start small:
- Attend an online event first
- Attend a local event in your city
- Go with a group of friends
- Share your certificates and experience

Each successful trip builds trust for the next one.

---

## Script example

> "There is a national hackathon called Smart India Hackathon organized by the Ministry of Education. Teams from across India participate. I want to register with a team from college. The grand finale is in Bangalore for 2 days. The government provides food and accommodation. I have already checked with my professor and she said it is okay. I will go with my classmates (names). I have prepared a budget. Can I show you the website and previous year results?"

---

## If they still say no

- **Ask what would make them comfortable** — a guardian accompanying you? A letter from college? A video call with organizers?
- **Try a smaller event first** — attend an online hackathon or a one-day event in your city
- **Involve a teacher** — a professor's recommendation goes a long way with Indian parents
- **Accept gracefully** — do not fight. Prove responsibility over time.

---

## Other tips

- Start the conversation early, not the night before registration closes
- Be calm and factual, not emotional
- Offer a "pilot trip" — one small event to prove yourself
- Share your certificate and experience after the event
- For recurring events, show you managed your time well the previous time
