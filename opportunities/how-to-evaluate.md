# How to Evaluate Opportunities

Not every opportunity is worth your time. This guide helps you assess whether a given hackathon, fellowship, competition, or program is worth applying to — and worth attending.

## The opportunity evaluation framework

Score each opportunity on these 5 criteria (1-5 scale):

### 1. Signal value (will this help my career?)
- 5 = Widely recognized program (SIH winner, Google Summer of Code, KVPY fellow)
- 4 = Well-regarded in specific circles (regional hackathon with good reputation)
- 3 = Decent for resume but not widely known
- 2 = Minimal external recognition
- 1 = No recognition or questionable reputation

### 2. Learning value (will I grow?)
- 5 = Intensive learning experience, mentorship, workshops
- 4 = High hands-on building time
- 3 = Moderate learning, some guided sessions
- 2 = Mostly passive (talks only, no building)
- 1 = Nothing new to learn

### 3. Network value (who will I meet?)
- 5 = Industry leaders, top mentors, fellow high-quality participants
- 4 = Good mix of peers and professionals
- 3 = Some interesting participants
- 2 = Mostly strangers, no structured networking
- 1 = No networking opportunities

### 4. Opportunity cost (what am I missing?)
- 5 = Low commitment (weekend, minimal prep)
- 4 = Moderate commitment (few days, some prep)
- 3 = Significant commitment (week, travel needed)
- 2 = Heavy commitment (multiple weeks, extensive prep)
- 1 = Conflicts with important academic/personal priorities

### 5. Logistics (can I actually do this?)
- 5 = Free to apply, travel covered, accommodation provided
- 4 = Low cost, easy to reach
- 3 = Moderate cost and effort
- 2 = Expensive or difficult to reach
- 1 = Cannot make it work

## Interpreting the score

| Total (out of 25) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 20-25 | Strong yes. Apply immediately. |
| 15-19 | Good opportunity. Apply if it fits your schedule. |
| 10-14 | Average. Apply only if you have spare time. |
| 5-9 | Weak. Skip unless it is your only option. |
| Below 5 | Skip. Your time is better spent elsewhere. |

## Red flags (skip these)

- **You have to pay to apply** — Legitimate opportunities do not charge application fees
- **Vague promises** — "Guaranteed internship", "certificate of excellence", "industry exposure" with no specifics
- **No track record** — First-year event with no known organizers, partners, or past editions
- **Poor communication** — Broken website, typos in official communication, unresponsive organizers
- **Too good to be true** — "Win ₹10 lakhs with zero effort" energy
- **Data harvesting** — Asking for excessive personal information (Aadhaar, PAN, passport) before selection
- **Pyramid energy** — "Bring 10 friends and get a bonus" or "pay to unlock higher tiers"

## Green flags (prioritize these)

- **Clear selection criteria** — You know exactly what they are looking for
- **Past edition data** — You can find previous winners, projects, or participant experiences
- **Transparent timeline** — Application dates, selection dates, event dates all published
- **Strong organizers** — Backed by known organizations, companies, or government bodies
- **Free + supportive** — Free to apply, travel reimbursement, good food and accommodation
- **Learning-focused** — Workshops, mentorship, feedback sessions included
- **Post-event value** — Alumni network, ongoing community, certification that carries weight

## How to research an opportunity

1. **Google "event name review"** or "event name experience" — find participant blogs or Reddit posts
2. **Check LinkedIn** — search for past participants and see where they landed
3. **Look at past winners** — does the quality of past winners match your level? Are they impressively better or similar to you?
4. **Ask in communities** — post in relevant Discord/Telegram groups: "Has anyone done X? Is it worth it?"
5. **Check the organizers** — are they established? Have they run events before?

## The opportunity cost trap

The biggest mistake students make is applying to too many things. Each application takes 1-5 hours. Each event takes 2-7 days including travel and recovery. If you attend 2 events per month, you lose 4-14 days per month. Choose wisely.

A good rule: for every 10 opportunities you see, apply to 3. For every 3 you get into, attend 1-2.

## Special case: First opportunity

If this is your first opportunity ever, lower the threshold. The first one is about learning the process, not about the signal value. Score of 12+ is fine for a first attempt.
