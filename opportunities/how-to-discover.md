# How to Discover Opportunities

Most students miss opportunities not because they are not qualified, but because they do not know where to look. This is a practical guide to finding fellowships, programs, competitions, and events that fit you.

## The discovery system

Effective opportunity discovery is a habit, not a one-time search. Set up a process and run it weekly.

## Tier 1: Core platforms (check weekly)

These platforms aggregate the most Indian student opportunities:

| Platform | What it lists | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| [Unstop](https://unstop.com) | Hackathons, competitions, fellowships, internships | All students |
| [Devfolio](https://devfolio.co) | Hackathons | Builders, developers |
| [LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com) | Fellowships, programs, events | Professional development |
| [Dare2Compete](https://dare2compete.com) | Competitions, hackathons, case studies | Business and tech students |
| [IamWire](https://iamwire.com) | Startup competitions, innovation challenges | Student founders |

Set 15 minutes every Sunday to scan these platforms. Save or bookmark anything interesting.

## Tier 2: Niche sources (check monthly)

Depending on your interest area:

| Interest | Sources |
|---|---|
| Research | INSPIRE, Kishore Vaigyanik Protsahan Yojana (KVPY), Indian Academy of Sciences fellowships |
| Policy/Public service | YLAC (Young Leaders for Active Citizenship), PRS Legislative Research internships |
| Social impact | Villgro, Yunus Social Business, Teach For India fellowships |
| Entrepreneurship | Atal Innovation Mission, i5 (startup India), NASSCOM 10,000 Startups |
| Open source | Google Summer of Code, Outreachy, MLH Fellowship |
| Design | Figma Community, Dribbble meetups, Adobe Design Circle |
| Writing | Youth Ki Awaaz, The Bastion fellowships, BMC (Blogging My City) |

## Tier 3: Your personal network (ongoing)

The best opportunities are often not listed anywhere. They come through:
- Seniors who attended events before you
- Professors with industry connections
- Discord and Telegram groups of hackathon communities
- WhatsApp groups of college clubs
- Alumni networks on LinkedIn

Build relationships with 2-3 senior students who are active in areas you care about. Ask them: "What is coming up that I should know about?"

## Tier 4: Google alerts and newsletters

Set up a Google Alert for terms like:
- "hackathon India 2026"
- "student fellowship India 2026"
- "innovation challenge India"
- "college competition [your city]"

Subscribe to newsletters:
- Unstop newsletter
- Devfolio newsletter
- Your college TPO/placement newsletter
- NIRF-ranked college newsletters (many list open opportunities)

## The shortlist process

From everything you find, shortlist by:
1. **Eligibility**: Do you meet the criteria? (class/year, marks, age)
2. **Timeline**: Can you make the deadline and time commitment?
3. **Interest**: Does this genuinely excite you?
4. **Value**: What will you gain even if you do not win?
5. **Cost**: Is it free to apply? Is travel reimbursed?

Keep a running shortlist of 5-10 opportunities. Apply to 2-3 at any given time. Do not overapply — quality matters more than quantity.

## Tracking tool

Create a simple tracker (Notion, Google Sheets, or paper):

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Opportunity name | SIH 2026 |
| Type | Hackathon |
| Source | Unstop |
| Deadline | 15 Aug 2026 |
| Eligibility | College students, team of 4 |
| Status | Applied |
| Notes | Need to find 3 teammates |
| Link | https://... |

Review this tracker weekly. Move deadlines above the current date to "Archived" or "Expired".

## What most students miss

- **Recurring patterns**: Many events happen at the same time every year. If you missed the 2025 cycle, set a calendar reminder for the 2026 cycle.
- **Early-bird advantages**: Some programs have rolling admissions. Applying early improves chances.
- **Smaller events**: Regional hackathons and college-level competitions are less competitive but equally valuable. Do not ignore them.
- **Non-obvious programs**: AI, design, and business competitions are less searched than coding competitions. Higher chance of selection.
