# AI for Students — A Practical Guide

AI is changing how students can learn, build, and create. This guide is a grounded, beginner-friendly overview.

## What AI means for you (not hype)

Most students do not need to build an AI model from scratch. The practical skills are:
- Using existing AI tools effectively
- Understanding what AI can and cannot do
- Building applications that use AI APIs
- Knowing enough to spot opportunities

## Key skills for students

| Skill | Why it matters | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt engineering | Get useful output from LLMs | Writing, coding, research |
| AI APIs (OpenAI, Gemini, Claude) | Add AI features to projects | Hackathons, portfolios |
| RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) | Make AI use your own data | Advanced projects |
| Fine-tuning basics | Customize models for specific tasks | ML competitions |
| Evaluation | Know if AI output is correct | Production-grade work |

## What AI is actually good for

- **Writing assistance** — drafts, editing, formatting
- **Code generation** — boilerplate, debugging, explanations
- **Research** — summarizing papers, finding patterns
- **Brainstorming** — generating ideas, exploring angles
- **Data analysis** — cleaning, visualizing, interpreting
- **Learning** — explaining concepts in different ways
- **Translation** — between languages and technical levels

## What AI is not good for

- **Facts it was not trained on** — hallucinations happen
- **Complex reasoning** — multi-step logic often fails
- **Original research** — it cannot discover new knowledge
- **Subjective judgment** — taste, design, ethics
- **Consistency** — same prompt can give different answers

## AI for hackathons

AI tools can give you a significant speed advantage:
- Use an LLM to scaffold a project quickly
- Use AI-powered search (Perplexity, Gemini) for research
- Use AI coding tools (Cursor, Copilot) for boilerplate
- Use AI design tools (Figma AI, Canva AI) for UI

**Important:** Know what the tool does. Do not submit AI-generated code you do not understand — judges will ask.

## The real opportunity

The biggest opportunity is not "learning AI" — it is building things with AI. Students who ship projects that use AI effectively will stand out more than students who just study AI theory.

AI moves fast. Tools that exist today may be obsolete in 6 months. Focus on fundamental skills (prompting, APIs, evaluation) rather than memorizing tool-specific workflows.
