# AI for Research

How Indian students can use AI tools to support academic and project research — not as a shortcut, but as a research assistant.

## What AI does well for research

- Summarizing papers and articles (save hours of reading)
- Explaining unfamiliar concepts in simpler terms
- Finding connections between different sources
- Suggesting related papers or approaches
- Helping structure your own writing and arguments

## What AI does poorly for research

- Citing real sources (AI invents citations — always verify)
- Giving accurate information about recent events (knowledge cutoffs exist)
- Understanding niche Indian-context topics (schemes, regional specifics, local policies)
- Representing minority or counter-mainstream viewpoints
- Making nuanced judgments about research quality

## Practical research workflow

1. **Search phase**: Use Google Scholar, Google, and your university library portal first. Do not start with AI.
2. **Summarize phase**: Paste paper abstracts or sections into an AI tool. Ask for a summary in 3 bullet points. Ask what methodology was used. Ask what the limitations are.
3. **Connect phase**: Ask the AI how this paper relates to others you have read. Ask for contrasting viewpoints. Ask what questions remain unanswered.
4. **Structure phase**: Use AI to outline your literature review or paper structure. Then write the content yourself. AI outlines are good starting points; AI-written content requires heavy editing.
5. **Verify phase**: Check every claim the AI made against real sources. Assume AI is wrong until you confirm otherwise.

## Specific use cases for Indian students

### Understanding a new domain for a hackathon
You have 48 hours to build something in healthcare/fintech/agritech. You do not know the domain. Use AI to:
- Explain the key problems in that domain
- List existing solutions and their limitations
- Suggest what a useful hackathon project could look like
- Explain regulatory considerations in India

### Writing project proposals
For government schemes (SSIP, i5, SIH), you need project proposals. Use AI to:
- Structure the proposal into problem/solution/impact
- Suggest metrics for measuring success
- Identify potential implementation challenges
- Generate a draft budget structure

### Preparing for competition presentations
- Use AI to simulate Q&A: ask it to act as a judge and ask you tough questions
- Use AI to review your presentation script for clarity and impact
- Use AI to suggest what audience questions might come up

### Academic research for papers
- Literature review organization
- Methodology description drafting
- Related work section structuring
- Identifying gaps your paper could address

## Tools to consider

| Tool | Best for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | General research, summarization, brainstorming | Free tier available |
| Claude (this tool) | Long document analysis, nuanced understanding | Free tier available |
| Google Gemini | Google Workspace integration, search-connected | Free |
| Perplexity | Research with citations (more reliable than pure LLMs) | Free tier available |
| Elicit | Academic paper search and analysis | Free tier limited |
| Consensus | Finding consensus in academic literature | Free tier limited |
| Connected Papers | Visualizing related academic papers | Free |
| NotebookLM | Organizing research from your own sources | Free |

## Red lines (do not do these)

- Do not submit AI-written assignments or papers as your own work
- Do not use AI for take-home exams or tests where it is prohibited
- Do not cite AI-generated sources without verifying them
- Do not paste confidential or personal data into public AI tools
- Do not trust AI evaluations of your own work — it is usually too generous
