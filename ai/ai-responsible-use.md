# AI: Responsible Use for Students

AI tools are powerful, but using them carelessly can hurt your learning, your reputation, and your career. This is about using AI as a tool without becoming dependent on it.

## The core tension

AI makes you more productive in the short term. But if you rely on it too much, you skip the hard parts of learning — and those hard parts are where real understanding develops.

The goal is to use AI to accelerate learning, not to replace it.

## What responsible use looks like

- **AI as tutor**: Ask AI to explain things you do not understand. Do not ask it to do your work.
- **AI as editor**: Write your own draft, then ask AI to improve clarity, grammar, or structure.
- **AI as brainstorm partner**: Use AI to generate ideas, then evaluate them yourself.
- **AI as reviewer**: Ask AI to critique your work and find gaps.
- **AI as practice partner**: Use AI to simulate interviews, presentations, or debates.

## What irresponsible use looks like

- Copy-pasting AI output without reading or understanding it
- Submitting AI-generated code without testing or understanding it
- Using AI to complete assignments that are meant to test your own understanding
- Never writing anything from scratch anymore
- Losing the ability to think through problems because AI does it for you

## The skill erosion risk

If you use AI to write all your code, you will not learn to debug. If you use AI to write all your essays, you will not learn to structure arguments. These skills matter in the long run. In interviews, in real projects, and in your career, you will need to demonstrate abilities that AI cannot substitute.

## When to avoid AI entirely

- Learning a new concept for the first time — try to understand it without AI first
- Coding interviews and assessments — practice without AI
- Writing exams and tests
- Early stages of learning a programming language
- Any situation where the process of figuring things out is the point

## How to detect unhealthy dependency

You might be overly dependent on AI if:
- You feel anxious when you cannot access AI tools
- You open a blank file and immediately reach for AI instead of starting yourself
- You cannot explain your own code or writing without referring to AI output
- You spend more time prompting AI than thinking about the problem
- Your AI-free output quality has noticeably declined over time

## For hackathons and competitions

During hackathons, using AI for boilerplate code, documentation, and debugging is normal and accepted. But:
- Understand every line of code AI generates for you
- Be ready to explain and defend AI-generated parts during judging
- Do not use AI to replace the core innovation of your project
- Some competitions have specific rules about AI usage — read them

## A personal rule

Before asking AI to do something, spend 5 minutes trying to do it yourself. If you are stuck, write down what you tried. Then use AI to unblock yourself. This way, AI becomes a tool for learning, not a crutch.
