# Hackathons

Hackathons are time-bound events where participants collaborate to build solutions — usually software or hardware prototypes — for specific problem statements or themes. In India, hackathons range from national government initiatives to campus-level events.

## Why hackathons matter

- Build project experience outside coursework
- Learn to work under pressure and in teams
- Get exposure to real-world problem statements from industry and government
- Network with peers, mentors, and recruiters
- Win prizes, recognition, and sometimes job offers
- Strengthen your portfolio and resume

## Types of hackathons in India

| Type | Example | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Government-led national hackathons | Smart India Hackathon | 36 hours (grand finale) |
| Corporate hackathons | Flipkart Grid, HSBC Hackathon | 24-48 hours |
| College fest hackathons | IIT Techfest, college fests | 24-36 hours |
| Online platform hackathons | Devfolio, HackerEarth, Unstop | 1-7 days |
| Community hackathons | MLH Localhost, GDG events | 1-2 days |

## How to choose the right hackathon

Not all hackathons are equally useful. Consider:
- **Your experience level**: Beginners should start with online or college-level hackathons. National-level events like SIH are better after you have attended 1-2 smaller events.
- **Your interest area**: Pick hackathons whose problem statements align with what you want to learn (AI, web, mobile, hardware, etc.)
- **Time commitment**: Weekend hackathons (24-48 hours) are manageable during college. Week-long hackathons require more planning.
- **Location**: Online hackathons are accessible to everyone. Offline hackathons offer better networking but require travel.
- **Prize vs learning**: If you are early in your journey, prioritize learning over prizes. Events with good mentorship and workshops are worth more than big prize pools.

## Strategy for first-timers

1. Start with an online hackathon on Devfolio or Unstop
2. Join with a team, even if you do not know them well
3. Build something simple that works rather than something complex that breaks
4. Focus on completing and presenting, not winning
5. After the event, document what you learned and what you would do differently

## Common hackathon mistakes

- **Over-scoping**: Trying to build too much in 24 hours. Fix: cut features aggressively. A working simple project beats a broken ambitious one.
- **Neglecting the presentation**: Spending all time coding, none on the pitch. Fix: leave 2 hours for presentation and demo preparation.
- **Poor team coordination**: Not dividing work clearly. Fix: assign roles (developer, designer, presenter) before starting.
- **Ignoring sleep**: Running on zero caffeine and adrenaline, crashing before the final presentation. Fix: take 2-3 hours of sleep.
- **Not talking to mentors**: Mentors exist to help. Use them. Fix: check in with a mentor at least twice during the event.
- **Forgetting to eat and hydrate**: Fix: set alarms for meals and water breaks.
