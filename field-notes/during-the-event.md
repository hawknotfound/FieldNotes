# During the Event: Field Notes

Observations about what actually happens during hackathons, competitions, and conferences — beyond the schedule.

## The first hour is the hardest

You arrive. You do not know where to sit. Everyone else looks like they know what they are doing. They do not. Find a spot, unpack your things, take a breath. The discomfort passes.

## Networking is awkward and that is fine

Talking to strangers is hard. The trick is not to have a perfect opener but to be genuinely curious. Ask what they are working on. Ask what brought them here. Most people are happy to talk about themselves. Listen more than you speak.

## Food and water logistics

- Meal times are announced but food often arrives late.
- Keep snacks with you at all times.
- Identify the water refill station within the first 30 minutes.
- If you have dietary restrictions, check at registration. Many events in India do not account for this well.

## Sleep strategy

In a 24-48 hour hackathon, you need at least 3-4 hours of sleep. The teams that sleep do better than the teams that power through. Find a quiet corner, set an alarm, take turns with your team.

## When your project breaks (it will)

Your demo will break. The internet will fail. The projector will not work. This is universal. The key is not to panic but to have a backup plan:
- Record a video demo beforehand
- Keep screenshots of key features
- Know your project well enough to explain it without the code running

## The middle hours are the real test

The first 6 hours are exciting. The last 6 hours are desperate. The middle hours — between hour 12 and hour 24 — are where most teams lose focus. This is when you need to push through. Take a walk. Get fresh air. Talk to another team. Reset and come back.

## Judging is unpredictable

You cannot control what judges value. Some look for technical complexity. Some look for social impact. Some look for a working prototype. Some look for a good presentation. Build something solid and present it clearly. The rest is out of your hands.

## Other participants are not competitors

The best part of events is not winning. It is meeting people who are as interested as you are in the same things. Exchange contacts. Follow up after the event. These connections last longer than any prize.

## The last hour chaos

Everything becomes urgent in the last hour. Submissions crash. Files do not upload. The printer runs out of paper. Finish everything 2 hours before the deadline. The last 2 hours are for buffer.

## After results

If you win: celebrate briefly, then be gracious. If you do not win: it means nothing about your potential. Some of the best projects I have seen did not win. Judging is a snapshot, not an evaluation of your ability.
