# Before the Event: Field Notes

Observations from real experiences about what happens before you actually attend a hackathon, competition, or conference.

## Registration is not selection

You apply to 10 events. You get into 3. This is normal. Do not take rejections personally. Most events have limited slots and high demand. The ones that accept you are often better fits anyway.

## The pre-event panic

Around 2-3 days before the event, you will feel unprepared. This happens to everyone. You will wonder if your project idea is good enough, if your team is ready, if you even belong there. Trust the process. The anxiety fades within the first hour of arrival.

## Travel planning is always more complex than expected

- Trains get cancelled. Buses run late. Flights get delayed.
- Always arrive the night before, not the morning of.
- Have a backup route in mind.
- Save the event venue's exact location offline (Google Maps offline mode).

## What nobody tells you about packing

- Power bank is non-negotiable.
- A small notebook and pen are useful even in a digital world.
- Carry a water bottle — event venues often have water but not cups.
- Snacks that do not make noise or smell (dry fruits, granola bars).
- A light jacket or hoodie — venues are air-conditioned unevenly.

## The preparation paradox

You will spend hours preparing. Most of what you prepare will not be used. But the preparation itself builds confidence. The act of preparing matters more than the specific things you prepare.

## Sleep before, not during

Many students plan to stay up all night before travel. This is a mistake. The night before you leave, sleep properly. You will have enough sleepless nights during the event itself.

## Communication with the team

If you are going with a team, agree on the plan 48 hours before:
- Who is bringing what (laptop, charger, extension cord, hardware)
- Meeting point and time
- What happens if someone misses the train/bus
- Who has the event tickets/confirmations
- Emergency contact numbers

## The impostor feeling starts early

Looking at the participant list or past winners will make you feel like you do not belong. This feeling will return multiple times. It is a signal that you are doing something new, not a signal that you should quit.
