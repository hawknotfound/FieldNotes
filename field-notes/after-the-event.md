# After the Event: Field Notes

What happens after the event is often more important than what happens during it.

## The post-event crash

You will feel exhausted and slightly empty after returning. This is normal. Your body and mind have been running at high intensity. Give yourself a day to rest before doing anything productive.

## Follow up within 48 hours

The connections you made lose value rapidly if you do not follow up. Within 48 hours:
- Connect on LinkedIn with a personal note mentioning where you met
- Share photos or highlights from the event
- If you promised to share something (code, resource, contact), do it immediately

## Document what happened

Write down what you learned while it is fresh. What went well? What would you do differently? What was surprising? This documentation is more valuable than the prize you may have won. It becomes the basis for your next attempt.

## Update your portfolio

- Add the project to GitHub with a proper README
- Write a LinkedIn post about your experience (tag the organizers, sponsors, team)
- Update your resume with the participation/win
- Add any new skills you picked up

## What you actually gained

You may feel like you did not achieve much. But you gained:
- Experience navigating a real-world event
- Practice working under pressure
- Exposure to how other people think and build
- Feedback on your work (even if not explicit)
- New contacts who share your interests

These compound over time. One event changes little. Ten events change everything.

## Dealing with the "what now" feeling

After a big event, regular life feels underwhelming. Classes feel slow. Daily routines feel pointless. This is temporary. Channel the energy into preparing for the next event. Use the momentum.

## Share back

The best thing you can do after gaining experience is to share it. Tell juniors or classmates about your experience. Write a small guide. Answer questions from people who want to try. This reinforces your learning and builds your reputation.
