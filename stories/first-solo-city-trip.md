# Story: My First Solo Trip to a City for an Event

I had never traveled alone outside my city. Everywhere I went, my parents dropped me, or I was with school friends on a class trip.

But there was a hackathon in Bangalore. My college team had registered. Everyone else backed out last minute due to exams. I had two choices: skip it, or go alone.

I went alone.

## The preparation

Two weeks before:
- Booked train (sleeper class, 14 hours)
- Booked a Zostel dorm bed
- Downloaded offline Bangalore map
- Made a list of everything to pack
- Told my parents (after booking, so they could not say no)

My mom made me promise to call every 4 hours. I agreed.

## The journey

The train was delayed by 3 hours. I sat at the station, nervous, wondering if I made a mistake.

On the train, I met another student going to the same hackathon. We became friends over chai. That was my first lesson of solo travel: you are never really alone.

## Reaching Bangalore

I got down at Yesvantpur station at 6 AM. Used the Metro for the first time. Messed up the line and went in the wrong direction. Another passenger helped me correct my route.

By 8 AM, I had checked into Zostel, taken a shower, and was at the event venue.

## The hackathon

Without my team, I joined a random team looking for a developer. We built something decent. Did not win. But the experience of walking into a room of strangers and saying "I can help" was valuable in itself.

## The return

On the way back, I felt different. The city I was nervous about arriving in now felt familiar. I knew which Metro line to take. I knew where to find cheap food. I knew how to navigate a new place alone.

My mom was relieved when I got home. I could see she was proud too.

## What I learned

- Indian cities are not as scary as they seem
- People are generally helpful (especially students)
- Bangalore is expensive but manageable on a budget
- Solo travel teaches you things no classroom can
- Parents will worry, but doing it once makes them trust you more

## Tips if you are going solo for the first time

- Book everything in advance (train, stay, event registration)
- Keep digital and physical copies of important documents
- Share your location with someone at home
- Reach the venue during daytime
- Trust your instincts about people and places
- It will feel uncomfortable for the first few hours. It gets better.
