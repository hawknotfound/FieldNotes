# Story: My First Real Networking Moment

I used to think networking meant collecting LinkedIn connections. I had 500+ connections, most of whom I never spoke to.

Then I went to a college fest hackathon in Pune.

## The moment

During lunch, I ended up sitting next to a guy who was judging the AI track. He had a company ID from a well-known startup. I was too nervous to speak.

He asked me: "So, what are you building?"

I mumbled something about my project. He nodded and went back to his food.

Then I remembered something I had read: "Ask people about their work, not for advice."

I asked: "What is the hardest problem you are working on right now?"

His face lit up. He spent the next 15 minutes explaining a data pipeline issue his team was facing. I did not understand half of it. But I listened, asked questions, and said "that sounds really interesting" genuinely.

## The follow-up

After the event, I connected with him on LinkedIn. In my request, I mentioned the specific thing we discussed — the data pipeline problem.

He accepted within an hour. A week later, he messaged me about an internship opening at his company. I applied. I got an interview.

(I did not get the internship. But I got an interview — that was more than I had before.)

## What I learned about networking

- It is not about collecting connections. It is about one genuine conversation.
- Ask about their work, not for favors.
- Follow up with something specific, not a generic template.
- You do not need to be an expert to have a good conversation. Being interested is enough.
- The best time to follow up is within 24 hours.

## How I network now

- I aim for 2-3 good conversations per event, not 20 business card exchanges
- I ask: "What are you working on that excites you?"
- I send follow-ups within 24 hours
- I try to share something useful (an article, a tool, an opportunity) within the first month
- I do not keep score. I just try to be genuinely useful.

## The real takeaway

The guy I met at that lunch? We still talk occasionally. He has recommended me for opportunities twice. Not because I impressed him with my skills (I was a beginner), but because I showed genuine interest in what he was doing.

That is what networking actually is.
