# Story: What Confidence Actually Feels Like

I used to think confident people were born confident. That they never felt nervous before a presentation. That they always knew what to say.

Then I started going to events and realized: confident people are just people who have done the thing enough times that they know what to expect.

## My definition of confidence

Confidence is not "I will definitely win."
Confidence is "I have been in this situation before, and I handled it."

## How I built confidence (by accident)

### Step 1: I showed up even when scared

First hackathon: I was terrified. I knew nothing. But I showed up.
Result: I learned how hackathons work. Next time was less scary.

### Step 2: I did the same thing multiple times

First city trip: stressful.
Second city trip: less stressful.
Third city trip: I knew exactly what to pack, where to stay, how to find food.

Each repetition reduced the fear.

### Step 3: I failed and survived

I bombed a pitch. The judges asked questions I could not answer. I walked off stage feeling like everyone was judging me.

Nobody mentioned it. Nobody cared. I was the only one who remembered my failure a week later.

### Step 4: I prepared properly

The first time I felt genuinely confident was at my 4th hackathon. I had practiced the tech stack. I had prepared a template. I had a team I trusted.

Confidence came from preparation, not from personality.

## What confidence feels like (for me)

- Not "I will definitely win" but "I have a plan if things go wrong"
- Not "I know everything" but "I know how to figure it out"
- Not "I am not nervous" but "I am nervous and I will do it anyway"
- Not "I am better than others" but "I have done this before and I can do it again"

## Practical strategies that worked

| Strategy | How it helped |
|---|---|
| Prepare more than you think you need | Reduced anxiety |
| Do it badly the first time | Broke the perfection cycle |
| Watch others do it first | Learned the format |
| Say "I don't know" honestly | Removed pressure to perform |
| Focus on helping teammates | Distracted from self-doubt |

## The turning point

For me, the turning point was when I stopped asking "Am I good enough?" and started asking "What is the next step?"

Not "Can I win this hackathon?" but "What stack should I use?"
Not "Will I embarrass myself?" but "What slide should I prepare?"
Not "Am I confident?" but "What can I do right now?"

Confidence turned out to be just: **action without guarantee of success.**

## So if you are waiting to feel confident before you start...

You will be waiting forever.

Confidence does not come before the thing. It comes after. You have to do the thing first, badly, nervously, imperfectly. And then again. And then again.

And one day, you will realize that the thing that used to terrify you is now routine. That is what confidence actually feels like.
