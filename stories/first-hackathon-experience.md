# Story: My First Hackathon

I was in my second year of engineering. I had zero projects. Zero confidence. But a friend said, "Come with me to this hackathon, it will be fun."

I said yes without knowing what I was getting into.

## Before the event

We registered for a 24-hour online hackathon on Devfolio. Theme was "EdTech." My team had:
- Me — knew basic Python, no web dev experience
- My friend — could write HTML/CSS
- A senior — the only one who actually knew what he was doing

We spent the week before panicking. Watched YouTube tutorials at 2x speed. Downloaded templates. Messaged the senior "bhaiya kya karein" every hour.

## Day of the hackathon

**Hour 1:** We decided to build a doubt-solving platform for students (original, I know). The senior set up the project. I was assigned the "frontend" because nobody else wanted it.

**Hour 3:** I had copied some Bootstrap template and changed the colors. Felt like a hacker.

**Hour 8:** Everything broke. The senior stayed calm and fixed it in 20 minutes. I realized I knew nothing.

**Hour 16:** My friend fell asleep on the desk. I was running on chai and samosas. The backend was working. Frontend was ugly but functional.

**Hour 22:** We started the presentation. I had to present because the senior said "tumhe bolne ki practice karni chahiye." I stammered through the first minute, then got into rhythm.

**Hour 24:** Submitted. Slept for 14 hours.

## What happened next

We did not win. Not even close. Out of 200 teams, we were probably somewhere in the middle.

But something changed. I now knew:
- How to build something from scratch
- How to work in a team under pressure
- What APIs actually do
- That I needed to learn web development properly
- That hackathons are not scary — they are just intense study sessions

## A year later

I had done 5 more hackathons. Won 2. Built a portfolio of projects. Got an internship because of one hackathon project.

But that first hackathon? That was the most important one. Because that is where I stopped being afraid of not knowing things.

## What I would tell my past self

- You do not need to know everything. Just start.
- Your first hackathon is for learning, not winning.
- Ask stupid questions. Mentors are there for that.
- Sleep. Seriously. A 2-hour nap will save your next 10 hours.
- Submit something incomplete rather than nothing at all.
