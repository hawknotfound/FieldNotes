# Parent Discussion Checklist

This is not about persuading parents. It is about preparing yourself so the conversation goes well. Parents say no when they see uncertainty and risk. Your job is to demonstrate preparation and responsibility.

## Before the conversation

### Gather these details

Have answers ready for every question your parents might ask:

| Question | Your answer should include |
|---|---|
| What is this event? | Full name, type (hackathon/competition/workshop), duration |
| Who is organizing it? | Organizer name, their reputation, past editions |
| Where is it? | City, venue name, distance from home |
| When is it? | Exact dates, including travel days |
| How much will it cost? | Itemized breakdown (see below) |
| Who is going with you? | Names of teammates or friends attending |
| How will you travel? | Train/bus/flight number, departure/arrival times |
| Where will you stay? | Accommodation details (event-provided or self-arranged) |
| Is it safe? | Safety plan (see below) |
| What about college/studies? | How you will manage missed classes |
| What will you gain? | Skills, network, certificate, resume value |
| Do you have to pay to apply? | Application fee or free |
| Have others from your college attended? | Examples of previous participants |

### Prepare a cost breakdown

| Item | Estimated cost |
|---|---|
| Travel (round trip) | ₹ |
| Accommodation | ₹ |
| Food | ₹ |
| Local transport | ₹ |
| Registration (if any) | ₹ |
| Miscellaneous | ₹ |
| **Total** | **₹** |

Show that you have thought about money. If you are using your own savings or have found sponsored options, mention it.

### Prepare a safety plan

- How will you stay in touch? (daily call/video call at a fixed time)
- Who is the emergency contact at the event? (organizer name and phone number)
- Where will you be staying? (share exact address)
- What is your backup plan if something goes wrong?
- Share live location access (Google Maps)

### Prepare an academic plan

- List the classes you will miss
- Plan to get notes from classmates
- Plan to catch up within 1 week after returning
- If exams are near, do not attend. Prioritize academics.
- Offer to show your parents your academic plan in writing

## During the conversation

### Dos

- Start early. Do not bring it up 2 days before the event.
- Be calm and respectful. Do not argue.
- Present facts, not emotions. "I want to go because..." is weaker than "This event is organized by X. Past participants have gone on to Y. Here is my cost breakdown."
- Acknowledge their concerns. "I understand you are worried about safety. Here is my plan..."
- Offer compromises. "I will call every evening. I will share my location. I will catch up on studies within a week."
- Involve them in planning. "Can I show you the event website? Would you like to talk to the organizers?"
- If they want to meet the organizers or see the venue, facilitate that.

### Don'ts

- Do not say "Everyone else is going". It is not a reason.
- Do not get defensive or emotional.
- Do not hide details. If something is uncertain, say so and explain how you will handle it.
- Do not pressure them for an immediate answer. Give them time to think.
- Do not compare with siblings or friends' parents.

## Common parent concerns and responses

### "It is too expensive."

Response: Present the cost breakdown. Offer to:
- Use your own savings for part of it
- Find travel reimbursement or sponsorship
- Reduce costs by traveling in a group or staying in budget accommodation

### "It is not safe."

Response: Present your safety plan in detail. Offer to:
- Share live location
- Call at fixed times
- Provide organizer contact information
- Show past edition photos or participant testimonials
- Travel with a group

### "You will miss classes."

Response: Present your academic plan. Offer to:
- Get notes from classmates
- Complete assignments before leaving
- Catch up within 1 week of return
- Show your academic calendar with the event dates clearly marked

### "These events are a waste of time."

Response: Explain what you will gain:
- Specific skills you will learn
- People you will meet
- How this connects to your career goals
- Past participant outcomes (internships, jobs, fellowships)
- Certificate or recognition

### "Who is organizing this? I have never heard of them."

Response: Show:
- Organizer website
- Past edition information
- LinkedIn profiles of organizers (if available)
- Articles or news about the event
- Participant testimonials

## After the conversation

- Thank them for listening, regardless of the outcome.
- If they said yes:
  - Share your travel plan, accommodation details, and contact information
  - Set up a daily check-in schedule
  - Follow through on your promises (call daily, catch up on studies)
- If they said no:
  - Ask what would make them comfortable
  - Offer to revisit the conversation after addressing their concerns
  - Look for alternative events that are closer, cheaper, or shorter
  - Do not attend against their wishes — it damages trust and future opportunities

## Decision checklist for yourself

Before initiating the conversation:
- [ ] I have complete event details (name, organizer, dates, venue, cost)
- [ ] I have a cost breakdown ready
- [ ] I have a safety plan ready
- [ ] I have an academic plan ready
- [ ] I have prepared for likely parent concerns
- [ ] I am calm and ready to listen
- [ ] I have given them enough notice (2+ weeks before the event)
