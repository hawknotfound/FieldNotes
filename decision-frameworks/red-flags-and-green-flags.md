# Red Flags and Green Flags

A 2-minute filter before you invest time in any opportunity.

## Green flags

| Flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Organizer is a known entity (government body, university, established company, well-known community) | Accountability. They have reputation to protect. |
| Past edition information available | You can verify quality, find past participants, see past projects. |
| Clear schedule published before registration | They have planned the event properly. |
| Judging criteria published | You know what they value — you can prepare. |
| Free to apply | No financial barrier. Low risk to try. |
| Travel support provided (reimbursement, accommodation, or both) | They value participants enough to invest. |
| Transparent selection process | You know how and why participants are chosen. |
| Responsive communication (email/Social confirmed within 48 hours, questions answered) | They respect participants. |
| Code of conduct published | They care about participant safety and experience. |
| Mentor office hours or guidance sessions available | Learning is prioritized, not just competition. |
| Alumni or past participants visible and accessible on LinkedIn | You can ask real people about their experience. |

## Red flags

| Flag | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|
| Application fee required | Legitimate opportunities do not charge you to apply. Some charge nominal fees for organized olympiads (SOF level), but be skeptical of any program asking for payment before selection. |
| Vague or missing organizer information | "Powered by X in association with Y" where X and Y have no online presence. No website. No LinkedIn. No track record. |
| Unrealistic promises | "Guaranteed internship", "100% placement", "Win ₹10 lakhs guaranteed", "Become a millionaire in 30 days". |
| Deadline extensions announced repeatedly | Suggests they are struggling to find participants. Quality programs have fixed deadlines. |
| Poor communication | Auto-generated emails with typos. No phone number. No direct contact person. Emails go unanswered for days. |
| Excessive personal information requested before selection | Asking for Aadhaar, PAN, passport, bank details, or full address during registration (before you are selected). They do not need this for an application. |
| "Bring your friends" bonus structure | "Get 10 friends to register and win a bonus". This is a pyramid-style acquisition tactic, not a quality program. |
| No selection process at all | "Everyone who applies gets in". This is not always bad (hackathons often accept everyone), but for fellowships and competitive programs, no selection means low peer quality. |
| Social media following is suspicious (bought followers, no engagement, generic comments) | Check their Instagram/LinkedIn. 10k followers but 2 likes per post is a red flag. |
| Organizer asks you to pay for "certificate" or "participation confirmation" | A certificate is an outcome of participation, not something you buy. |
| Event date keeps changing | Unprofessional planning. May get cancelled. |
| No physical address or registered office mentioned | For government schemes and registered companies, this should be publicly available. |

## How to investigate

1. **Google "[opportunity name] review"** — find participant experiences
2. **Check LinkedIn** — search for past participants. Message 2-3 of them. Ask: "How was your experience?"
3. **Check the organizer's LinkedIn/website** — do they look legitimate? How long have they existed?
4. **Search for "[opportunity name] scam" or "[opportunity name] fake"** — if there are complaints, they will show up
5. **Ask in communities** — post in Discord/Telegram/WhatsApp groups: "Has anyone done this program? Is it legit?"

## When to skip without further analysis

If ANY of these are true, skip the opportunity entirely:

- [ ] Application fee required with no government backing
- [ ] Organizer has no verifiable online presence
- [ ] Promises guaranteed money, job, or internship with no selection criteria
- [ ] Asks for Aadhaar/PAN/bank details during registration (before selection)
- [ ] Multiple participants report it as a scam or waste of time (verified)
