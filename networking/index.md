# Networking

How to build genuine professional connections as a student in India — at events, online, and within communities.

## What this covers

- How to network at events (hackathons, conferences, competitions)
- Indian tech and professional communities worth joining
- Building an online presence (LinkedIn, GitHub, Twitter)
- Approaching mentors and industry professionals
- Following up effectively

## Files in this folder

## Core principles

1. **Help others before asking for help** — share what you know
2. **Be specific** — "I am interested in ML and built X project" is better than "I want to learn"
3. **Follow up** — the value of networking is in the follow-up, not the first conversation
4. **Quality over quantity** — 10 genuine connections are worth more than 100 random ones
5. **Give before you take** — share resources, answer questions, be useful

## Building your LinkedIn presence

Your LinkedIn profile is your public-facing professional identity. For students:
- **Profile photo**: A clear, well-lit headshot (not a selfie, not a group photo)
- **Headline**: Instead of "Student at XYZ College", write "Building [thing] | Interested in [area] | [X] hackathons participated"
- **About section**: 3-4 lines about what you build, what you are learning, and what you are looking for
- **Featured**: Pin your best project, hackathon win, or a post you wrote
- **Activity**: Like and comment on posts from people in your field. Share your own updates when you build something

Post once a week about something you learned or built. Do not worry about perfection. Consistency matters more than polish.

## How to approach mentors

Mentors are busy people. Respect their time:

1. **Do your homework first**: Before asking for help, try to solve the problem yourself. Mention what you tried.
2. **Be specific**: "I am building X and stuck on Y. I tried A and B. Can you suggest how to approach C?" works better than "Can you mentor me?"
3. **Offer value**: Share what you learned from their advice. Mention how you applied it. This makes mentors want to help you again.
4. **Do not ask "Can I pick your brain?"**: It is vague. Instead, ask a specific question.
5. **Follow up**: If a mentor spends 15 minutes on a call with you, send a thank-you note and an update a month later.

## Following up effectively

Most connections fade because nobody follows up. Here is a simple system:

- **Within 24 hours**: Send a LinkedIn connection request with context ("Great meeting you at React Bangalore. Your talk on X was really helpful.")
- **Within 1 week**: Send a message referencing something specific from your conversation ("I tried the approach you suggested for Y and it worked. Thanks!")
- **Within 1 month**: Share something relevant (article, project update, event) that reminds them of who you are
- **Every 3-6 months**: Check in with a specific update or question

The goal is to be memorable, not annoying. One meaningful interaction every few months is enough.

## Networking at events (hackathons, conferences, competitions)

- The best time to network is during meals, tea breaks, and after talks — not during sessions
- Start with observation: listen to conversations before joining
- Ask open-ended questions: "What brought you here?", "What are you working on?", "What did you think of the talk?"
- Exchange contacts before you leave, not at the very end
- After the event, send a group message to people you met if you connected with multiple people

## Online communities worth joining

- Discord servers of hackathons you attend or want to attend
- Telegram groups for specific technologies (Python India, React India, etc.)
- Reddit: r/developersIndia, r/JEENEETards, specific programming language communities
- LinkedIn groups for campus ambassadors and student programs
- College-specific WhatsApp groups, club Discords
