# First Opportunity Roadmap

Go from "never participated in anything" to "successfully completed my first event."

**Who this is for**: Students who have zero experience with hackathons, competitions, fellowships, or any extracurricular program. You have no portfolio. No network. No idea where to start.

**Timeframe**: 3-5 weeks from zero to completed participation.

## Phase 1: Discover (Week 1)

### Step 1.1: Figure out what you actually want to do
Write down answers to these three questions. Be honest, not aspirational:
- What do I enjoy doing in my free time? (not what you think you should enjoy)
- What am I okay at? (not what you are great at — just baseline competent)
- How much time can I actually commit per week? (not "as much as needed" — real number)

### Step 1.2: Search for opportunities
Use these sources in this order:
1. **Unstop** — search by "beginner friendly" or "no experience required"
2. **Devfolio** — look for upcoming hackathons
3. **College WhatsApp/Telegram groups** — ask seniors "what events have you participated in?"
4. **LinkedIn** — search "[your field] hackathon India 2026" or "[your field] competition India 2026"

Do not search for more than 2 hours total. You are looking for 5 potential matches, not every opportunity that exists.

### Step 1.3: Shortlist to 3
Filter your 5 down to 3 where:
- You meet all eligibility criteria (class, age, marks — do not ignore this)
- The deadline is at least 7 days away
- The time commitment fits your schedule
- You are actually interested (not just because it looks good on a resume)

## Phase 2: Evaluate (Week 1-2)

### Step 2.1: Quick safety check
Before spending time, run through [red-flags-and-green-flags.md](../decision-frameworks/red-flags-and-green-flags.md). If any major red flag appears, remove this opportunity from your list.

### Step 2.2: Score each opportunity
Use [opportunity-evaluation-framework.md](../decision-frameworks/opportunity-evaluation-framework.md). Score each of your 3 shortlisted opportunities. Pick the one with the highest score.

For your first time, prioritize:
- **Learning value** (workshops, mentorship → good)
- **Low barrier** (free, online, beginner-friendly → good)
- **Time commitment** (weekend or less → good)

Do not prioritize:
- Prize money
- Prestige of the organizer
- What your friends think

## Phase 3: Prepare (Week 2-3)

### Step 3.1: Register and confirm
- Complete registration immediately after deciding
- Save the confirmation email/screenshot
- Join the event Discord/Telegram/WhatsApp group
- Read all event communications thoroughly

### Step 3.2: Gather what you need
- Create or update your LinkedIn profile (basic photo, headline, about section)
- If it is a hackathon: install required tools (VS Code, Git, Python/Node.js)
- If it is a competition: understand the format, rules, and judging criteria
- If it is a fellowship: prepare application essays or documents
- Gather any documents needed (ID, resume, transcripts)

### Step 3.3: Prepare logistically
- Mark dates on your calendar
- If travel is involved: book travel and accommodation
- Inform parents and get permission if needed
- Prepare your bag (see [event-readiness-checklist.md](../decision-frameworks/event-readiness-checklist.md))

### Step 3.4: Learn the basics
Spend 5-10 hours on preparation:
- For hackathons: learn enough of one technology to build something simple
- For competitions: solve 5-10 past problems
- For fellowships: research the organization and its work

Do not try to become an expert. You just need to be functional.

## Phase 4: Participate (Event day)

### Step 4.1: Arrive early and settle in
- Reach the event (online or offline) 15 minutes early
- For online: test your microphone, camera, internet
- For offline: identify washroom, food, water locations
- Find your spot and set up

### Step 4.2: Set small achievable goals
Your goals for the event:
1. Complete your submission or participation
2. Learn one thing you did not know before
3. Talk to one person you did not know before

That is it. Winning is not the goal. Doing all three is a success.

### Step 4.3: Work through the event
- Follow the schedule
- Ask for help when stuck (mentors, organizers, other participants)
- Take breaks. Eat. Hydrate.
- Do not compare yourself to others. Focus on your own participation.
- Submit or finish whatever you started.

## Phase 5: Follow-up (Post-event, 48 hours)

### Step 5.1: Rest
You will be tired. Take a day to recover.

### Step 5.2: Document everything
Write down while it is fresh:
- What did I actually do? (list the tasks)
- What did I learn? (skills, insights, surprises)
- What would I do differently next time? (be specific)
- Who did I meet? (add them on LinkedIn within 48 hours)
- What did I enjoy? What did I not enjoy?

Save this somewhere you can find it (Notion, Google Docs, notes app). You will reference it before your next event.

### Step 5.3: Update your portfolio
- Add the experience to your LinkedIn (Events section or Projects section)
- Push any code to GitHub with a README
- Write a short LinkedIn post about your experience
- Save any certificates or recognition

### Step 5.4: Follow up with people
- Connect with anyone you met on LinkedIn (add context: "met at [event]")
- Send a thank-you message to mentors or helpful participants
- Join the event alumni or community group if one exists

## Phase 6: Reflect and plan next (Week 4-5)

### Step 6.1: Review your documentation
Read what you wrote in Phase 5. What patterns do you see? What was harder than expected? What was easier?

### Step 6.2: Decide on next steps
- If you enjoyed it → find another event within 1-2 months
- If you did not enjoy it → try a different type of event
- If it was overwhelming → try a smaller or simpler event next time

### Step 6.3: Apply what you learned
Use the skills or knowledge from this event in your coursework, personal projects, or next event.

## Checkpoints

| Phase | Check | Pass/fail |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | I found 3 opportunities that match my eligibility and interest | |
| Evaluate | I scored them and picked the best one | |
| Prepare | I am registered, packed, and have basic skills ready | |
| Participate | I showed up, did my best, and submitted/finished | |
| Follow-up | I documented, updated portfolio, followed up | |
| Reflect | I decided what to do next | |

If you completed all phases, you went from zero to successfully participating. That is the goal.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Applying too late | Procrastination | Set a personal deadline 48 hours before official deadline |
| Over-preparing | Fear of being underqualified | Learn enough to start, then learn on the go |
| Not asking for help | Fear of looking dumb | Mentors exist to help you. Ask early. |
| Comparing with others | Social media / ego | Focus on your own goals. You are here to learn. |
| Not following up | Forgetting / laziness | Set a reminder 24 hours after the event |
| Quitting mid-event | Overwhelm or disappointment | Take a break, reset, come back. Finishing matters. |

## Handling rejection

You will be rejected from some events. That is not a reflection of your potential.

**If you are not selected:**
- It usually means too many applicants, not that you are unqualified
- Request feedback if the program offers it
- Apply to the next event immediately (do not wait)
- Keep building skills in the meantime

**If you participate but do not win:**
- Winning depends on many factors outside your control
- You still gained experience, skills, and connections
- Most successful people lost many times before winning

**If you mess up during the event:**
- Everyone makes mistakes at their first event
- What matters is that you finished
- The lessons from failure are more valuable than easy success

## Realistic outcomes

| What you will likely gain | What you will probably not gain |
|---|---|
| Experience of how events work | Fame or recognition |
| One item for your resume/portfolio | A job offer |
| 2-5 new connections | A large network |
| Clearer understanding of your interests | Complete clarity on your career |
| Knowledge of what to do differently next time | Perfect execution |
