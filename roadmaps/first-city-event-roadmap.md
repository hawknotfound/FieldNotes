# First City Event Roadmap

Go from "I have never traveled to an event in another city" to "I successfully attended an event and returned safely."

**Who this is for**: Students attending their first offline event in a different city. Includes special guidance for students from small towns, first-time solo travelers, and anyone nervous about the logistics.

## Phase 1: Is this event worth traveling for? (4 weeks before)

Before planning anything, confirm that traveling for this event makes sense.

Run through [travel-worthiness-checklist.md](../decision-frameworks/travel-worthiness-checklist.md). Score it. Only proceed if:
- The learning/networking value justifies the cost
- You can afford it without financial strain
- The dates do not conflict with exams or major commitments

If you are unsure, look for an event closer to home first.

## Phase 2: Plan the trip (3-4 weeks before)

### Step 2.1: Get permission early
- Inform your parents/family as soon as you decide
- Have a clear plan ready: event details, cost breakdown, safety plan
- See [parent-discussion-checklist.md](../decision-frameworks/parent-discussion-checklist.md) for preparation
- If your college requires permission, start the process immediately

### Step 2.2: Book travel
- **Trains**: Book on IRCTC at least 2-3 weeks ahead. Sleeper class is cheapest. 3AC is more comfortable.
- **Buses**: RedBus or IntrCity. Overnight buses save accommodation costs.
- **Flights**: Only if distance >1,000 km and you found a deal (student discounts on some airlines).
- **Always book return travel at the same time.** Waiting until after the event is expensive and risky.

### Step 2.3: Book accommodation
Check in this order:
1. Does the event provide accommodation? → If yes, confirm and skip this step.
2. Do you have a friend or relative in the city? → Ask if you can stay.
3. Hostels (Zostel, GoStops, Hosteller) → Cheapest option, social atmosphere.
4. Budget hotels / OYO → Only if hostels are not available.

### Step 2.4: Calculate your budget

| Item | Estimated | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Travel (round trip) | ₹ | Include both ways |
| Accommodation | ₹ | Per night × number of nights |
| Food | ₹ | ₹300-500/day is reasonable |
| Local transport | ₹ | Metro, auto, bus |
| Contingency | ₹ | 20% buffer for unexpected expenses |

Total estimated: ₹_________

Can you afford this? If not:
- Can you reduce any costs? (groups travel cheaper, sharing rooms)
- Does the event reimburse travel or provide food?
- Can you earn or save this money before the event?

### Step 2.5: Prepare documents
- Event confirmation (printed or saved offline)
- College ID card
- Aadhaar card or other government ID
- Emergency contact numbers (organizers, accommodation, family)
- Medical information (allergies, conditions, doctor's number)

## Phase 3: Special guidance for first-time situations

### If you are from a small town traveling to a big city

**What will feel different:**
- Everything is farther. A 30-minute commute in a small town is a 2-hour commute in a city.
- Public transport is more complex. Learn the metro/bus routes before you arrive.
- People move faster. They talk faster, walk faster, expect faster responses.
- Language may be different. In Bangalore/Kolkata/Chennai, learn a few basic local phrases.
- Costs are higher. A meal that costs ₹50 in your town costs ₹150 in the city.

**How to prepare:**
- Watch YouTube videos about the city's public transport system
- Download offline Google Maps of the area around the venue and your accommodation
- Learn the basic layout: where is the railway station, venue, and your stay on the map
- Save the venue address in multiple languages if needed (map search, text, screenshot)
- Arrive 1 day early if possible — gives you time to orient yourself

**What to expect from other participants:**
- Many will be from bigger cities and have more exposure
- They will use English or Hinglish more fluently in professional settings
- Some may seem intimidatingly confident — this is a surface-level impression
- You belong there as much as anyone. Selection was based on merit.

### If you are attending solo

**Before the event:**
- Join the event Discord/Telegram and message a few participants beforehand
- If the event has room-sharing, opt in — shared rooms are safer and less lonely
- Tell your family your exact schedule and check-in times
- Share your live location with a family member

**At the event:**
- Stay in public areas. Do not isolate yourself in your room.
- Sit near other people, not in a corner.
- Introduce yourself to your neighbors. "First time here? Me too." is a great opener.
- Keep your belongings with you at all times.
- Trust your instincts about people. If someone seems off, create distance.

### If you are an introvert or socially anxious

- Set a small goal: "I will talk to 2 new people today."
- Prepare 2-3 conversation starters: "What brought you here?", "What are you working on?", "Is this your first time at this event?"
- It is okay to spend some time alone. But push yourself to interact at least a few times.
- The first conversation is the hardest. After that, it gets easier.

## Phase 4: Packing (1-2 days before)

### Essentials
- [ ] Laptop and charger (extension cord if sharing with team)
- [ ] Phone charger and power bank (10,000+ mAh minimum)
- [ ] College ID + one additional ID (Aadhaar, driving licence)
- [ ] Event confirmation (printed or offline on phone)
- [ ] Water bottle (reusable)
- [ ] Notebook and pen

### Comfort
- [ ] Light jacket or hoodie (venues and AC buses are cold)
- [ ] Earphones/headphones + eye mask (for sleeping on transport)
- [ ] Slippers/flip-flops (for hostels)
- [ ] Small towel and basic toiletries
- [ ] Paracetamol, ORS packets, band-aids
- [ ] Any personal medication

### Finance
- [ ] Cash (₹500-1,000 in small denominations for autos, tea, snacks)
- [ ] UPI enabled on phone
- [ ] At least one backup payment method

## Phase 5: Travel day

### Departure
- Leave home with 2x buffer time (traffic, delays, confusion)
- Share live location with family
- Confirm your return ticket is booked
- Keep your ID and ticket easily accessible (not buried in a bag)

### Arrival
- Go directly to your accommodation or the venue
- If arriving late, use app-based transport (Ola/Uber), not street autos
- Check in, drop your bags
- Locate the event venue (walk there if nearby, note the route)

### During the event
- Follow the [first-hackathon-roadmap.md](first-hackathon-roadmap.md) or [first-opportunity-roadmap.md](first-opportunity-roadmap.md) for participation guidance
- Keep your belongings within sight at all times
- Eat when food is provided (you do not know when the next meal is)
- Step outside for fresh air if you feel overwhelmed
- Call family once during the event to confirm you are safe

## Phase 6: Return and follow-up

### Return journey
- Leave the venue with enough buffer to reach station/airport
- Confirm your return transport is on time
- Share your return location with family
- On reaching home, confirm to family that you are back safely

### Post-event (within 48 hours)
- [ ] Rest. You will be tired. Take a day.
- [ ] Connect with people you met on LinkedIn
- [ ] Write down what you learned and what you would do differently
- [ ] Save any certificates or photos
- [ ] Calculate your actual expenses vs budget

### Post-event (within 1 week)
- [ ] Thank anyone who helped you (organizers, mentors, hosts)
- [ ] Share a LinkedIn post about your experience
- [ ] Update your portfolio or resume
- [ ] Decide on your next event

## Safety rules

- Share your live location with a family member at all times
- Save emergency contacts: event organizer, accommodation front desk, local police
- Do not accept drinks (water or otherwise) from strangers
- Do not leave your laptop or phone unattended
- If you feel unsafe, go to a public area and call someone you trust
- Late at night, use app-based transport only
- Trust your instincts. If a situation feels wrong, leave.

## Common first-trip mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Booking arrival for the morning of the event | Arrive the night before. Trains run late. |
| Not booking return travel | Return prices are higher and availability is lower. Book both ways together. |
| Overpacking | You will move around. Pack light. A small backpack is enough for 2-3 days. |
| Forgetting a power bank | Most common regret. Phone dies at the worst time. |
| Not informing family of plans | They worry. Share your schedule proactively. |
| Spending all money on day 1 | Keep a daily spending limit. Track expenses. |
| Staying isolated | The value of in-person events is the people. Talk to someone. |
