# Networking Roadmap

Learn to build genuine professional connections as a student — without being fake, pushy, or uncomfortable.

**Who this is for**: Students who find networking awkward, intimidating, or unnatural. If you think "networking" is a dirty word, start here.

## The real truth about networking

Most networking advice is designed for extroverts or people with existing confidence. This roadmap is for everyone else.

**What networking actually is**: Having normal conversations with people who share your interests, and staying in touch afterward.

**What networking is not**: Collecting business cards, sending generic LinkedIn requests, or "selling yourself."

If you can have a 2-minute conversation about something you are working on, you can network. That is the only skill you need.

## Phase 1: Fix your online presence (Week 1)

Before you talk to anyone, make sure people can find you.

### LinkedIn

| Element | What to do | What not to do |
|---|---|---|
| Photo | Decent quality, plain background, smiling | Selfie, group photo, no photo at all |
| Headline | "Building X | Interested in Y | Z hackathons" | "Student at XYZ College" only |
| About section | 3-4 lines: what you build, what you are learning, what you are looking for | Blank or "I am a passionate learner" |
| Featured | Pin your best project or a post you wrote | Nothing pinned |

**One-time setup**: 30 minutes. Do it once, update it every 3 months.

### GitHub (if you build things)
- Profile README with a short bio
- Pin your best 3-6 repositories
- Each repo should have a README that explains what it does

## Phase 2: Learn to talk about yourself (Week 1-2)

### Write a 30-second introduction

Template:
"Hi, I'm [name]. I'm a [year] student at [college]. I've been working on [project/interest] and I'm here because [reason]. What about you?"

That is it. You do not need a tagline or a personal brand.

### Practice this

- Say it out loud 5 times
- Say it to a friend
- Say it while recording yourself

The goal is not memorization. The goal is being able to say your name and what you do without hesitating.

## Phase 3: Low-stakes interactions (Week 2-3)

Do not start with in-person events. Build the habit online first.

### Step 3.1: Comment on posts
Find 5 posts from people in your field on LinkedIn. Comment with something specific:
- "Great post. The point about [X] resonated because [reason]."
- "Thanks for sharing. I have been exploring [related topic] and this helps."

Not: "Great post!" or "Thanks for sharing." Add value.

### Step 3.2: Send 3 DMs
Send a message to someone you follow but do not know:
"Hi [name], I've been following your work on [topic]. I recently started [your project/learning] and found your posts helpful. Thanks for sharing."

No request. No ask. Just appreciation. That is the foundation.

### Step 3.3: Join an online community
- Find a Discord, Telegram, or WhatsApp group for your field
- Introduce yourself in the #introductions channel
- Answer one question someone else asked
- Share one resource that helped you

## Phase 4: In-person networking (Week 3-6)

### Step 4.1: Before the event

**Prepare, do not script:**
- Know what you are working on (1-2 sentences)
- Know why you are at this event (1 sentence)
- Prepare 3 open-ended questions to ask others

**3 questions that always work:**
1. "What brought you to this event?"
2. "What are you working on these days?"
3. "What has been the most interesting part of the event for you?"

These work because they are genuine and get people talking about themselves.

### Step 4.2: During the event

**Who to talk to:**
- People standing alone (they are also looking for someone to talk to)
- People sitting near you
- Speakers or mentors during breaks
- Other participants at meals

**How to start conversations:**
- "Is this your first time at this event?" (works every time)
- "What do you think of the talks so far?"
- "I liked your question during the session..."
- "Hi, I'm [name]. I haven't talked to anyone yet, so I thought I would start with you." (honesty works)

**How to exit a conversation gracefully:**
- "It was great talking to you. I am going to grab some food. Let me know if you want to connect on LinkedIn."
- "I should prepare for my next session, but nice meeting you. Here is my LinkedIn."

No one is offended by a polite exit. Everyone understands events are busy.

### Step 4.3: What not to do

- Do not interrupt someone who is already deep in conversation
- Do not lead with "Can you give me a job/internship/opportunity?"
- Do not hand out your resume before having a conversation
- Do not try to talk to everyone. 5 meaningful conversations are better than 20 name exchanges.
- Do not stay in your comfort zone with people you already know

## Phase 5: Follow up (Within 48 hours)

This is where most people fail. The follow-up is more important than the first conversation.

### Step 5.1: LinkedIn request with context

Bad: "Hi, I would like to connect."

Good: "Hi [name], great meeting you at [event]. I really enjoyed your perspective on [topic]. I am working on [your thing] and would love to stay connected. Thanks!"

### Step 5.2: If you promised something, deliver it

- "I will share that article with you" → Share it within 24 hours
- "I will introduce you to [person]" → Do it within 48 hours
- "I will send you my project link" → Send it within 24 hours

### Step 5.3: Stay in touch (low effort, high value)

- Once a month: share something relevant with your new connections
- When you see their post: comment meaningfully
- When you achieve something: share it (and they will see it)

You do not need to message every week. Once every 1-2 months is enough to stay on someone's radar without being annoying.

## Phase 6: Building long-term relationships

The people who matter most are not the ones with the biggest titles. They are the ones you interact with regularly.

**How to identify valuable connections:**
- People who respond when you reach out
- People who share useful information
- People at a similar stage in their journey
- People who are generous with their knowledge

**How to BE a valuable connection:**
- Respond when someone reaches out to you
- Share useful information you find
- Be generous with what you know
- Follow through on promises

## Introvert-specific strategies

- **Set a quota**: "I will talk to 3 people today." Once you hit 3, you can stop.
- **Use the environment**: Ask about the event, the venue, the food. Low-stakes topics.
- **Pair with an extrovert**: If you know someone outgoing, stay near them. They will draw people in.
- **Take breaks**: If you feel drained, step outside for 10 minutes. Recharge.
- **Quality over quantity**: One deep conversation is better than 10 surface-level ones.

## Realistic outcomes table

| Effort level | Likely result |
|---|---|
| Attended event, talked to 1-2 people | One or two new LinkedIn connections |
| Talked to 5+ people, followed up with context | 3-5 meaningful connections that may lead to future opportunities |
| Followed up consistently over 6+ months | Network of 20-30 people who know what you do and think of you when opportunities arise |
| Built genuine relationships with peers at similar stages | Collaborations, team-ups, referrals, friendship |

## Common networking mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Asking for a job in the first conversation | Too forward, makes people defensive | Build the relationship first. Opportunities come later. |
| Generic connection requests | They will ignore or delete | Always add context about where you met |
| Talking too much about yourself | Boring for the other person | Ask questions. Listen 70% of the time. |
| Not following up | The connection dies immediately | Set a reminder for 24 hours after each event |
| Only networking upward | Ignoring peers who will grow with you | Build horizontal connections too |
| Trying to meet everyone | Superficial interactions, no depth | Focus on 5 good conversations per event |

## The networking habit

| Frequency | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Comment on 1-2 posts in your field | 5 min |
| Weekly | Post something (learning, project, question) | 10 min |
| Weekly | DM 1 person with a genuine comment or question | 5 min |
| Monthly | Attend one event (online or offline) | 1-3 hours |
| Monthly | Review your connections — who have you not talked to? | 10 min |
| Quarterly | Write a longer post or article | 1 hour |
