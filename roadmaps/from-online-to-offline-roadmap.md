# From Online to Offline: Roadmap

Most Indian students start with online participation — coding platforms, online courses, virtual events. The real growth comes from attending offline events. This roadmap bridges that gap.

## Why move offline?

- Deeper connections: online interactions are shallow; meeting in person builds real relationships
- Higher quality learning: offline hackathons and workshops teach more in 48 hours than months of self-study
- Visibility: being present makes you memorable to organizers, mentors, and recruiters
- Confidence: navigating real-world events builds skills that no online activity can replicate
- Fun: offline events are genuinely more enjoyable

## Phase 1: Build your foundation (Online)

Start online before going offline. You need basic competence to get value from offline events.

### Step 1: Participate in 2-3 online events
- Join an online hackathon (Devfolio, HackerEarth, MLH)
- Participate in an online coding competition (CodeChef, Codeforces)
- Attend a virtual workshop or webinar

### Step 2: Build something visible
- Create a project and put it on GitHub with a proper README
- Write about what you learned (LinkedIn post or blog)
- Share your work in relevant communities

### Step 3: Join online communities
- Discord servers of hackathons you want to attend
- LinkedIn groups for student developers
- College-specific groups or WhatsApp communities

### Step 4: Identify your first offline event
Pick one that:
- Is in your city or a nearby city (or has travel reimbursement)
- Has a beginner-friendly reputation
- Matches your skill level
- Falls during a break or long weekend

## Phase 2: Prepare for your first offline event

### Step 5: Plan the logistics
- Check dates against your academic calendar
- Book travel early (train/bus)
- Arrange accommodation (friends, hostels, or event-provided)
- Calculate budget and arrange funds
- Get permission from parents if needed (use [parents/guide](../parents/guide.md))

### Step 6: Prepare technically
- Brush up on skills relevant to the event
- Prepare a project idea (even if you do not use it)
- Pack your bag (laptop, charger, power bank, notebook, water bottle)
- Download offline maps of the event location

### Step 7: Prepare mentally
- Accept that you will feel uncomfortable. That is the point.
- Set small goals: talk to 3 new people, learn 1 new thing, share your work once
- Do not put pressure on winning. The goal is experience.

## Phase 3: At the event

### Step 8: Arrive early and settle in
- Reach the venue at least 30 minutes before registration
- Find your spot, set up, introduce yourself to neighbors
- Identify food, water, and washroom locations

### Step 9: Network intentionally
- Talk to at least 5 people you do not know
- Exchange contacts (LinkedIn, phone, WhatsApp)
- Ask questions: What are you working on? What brought you here?

### Step 10: Participate fully
- Work on your project. Present it. Accept feedback.
- Attend workshops or talks if available
- Talk to mentors and judges
- Stay until the end, even if your project is not winning

## Phase 4: After the event

### Step 11: Follow up within 48 hours
- Connect with people you met (add context in the request)
- Share photos and highlights on LinkedIn
- Send thank-you messages to helpful people

### Step 12: Document and reflect
- Write down 3 things you learned
- Write down 3 things you would do differently
- Update your portfolio with the project

### Step 13: Plan the next one
- The first offline event is the hardest. The second is easier.
- Target another event within 3 months
- Apply the lessons from this event

## Overcoming common barriers

| Barrier | Solution |
|---|---|
| No money for travel | Look for events with travel reimbursement; split costs with teammates |
| Parents do not allow | Share success stories; involve them in planning; show responsibility |
| Fear of going alone | Find a friend; join event Discord beforehand to find roommates |
| Impostor syndrome | Everyone starts somewhere; nobody knows everything |
| Academic schedule conflict | Choose events during holidays; plan submissions around exams |
| No skills yet | Start with beginner-friendly events; learn on the go |

## Signs you are ready for offline

- You have participated in at least 1 online event
- You have built at least 1 project on your own
- You are active in 2+ online communities
- You can afford (or arrange) the travel
- You are excited (and nervous) about meeting new people

If you hit 3/5, you are ready.
