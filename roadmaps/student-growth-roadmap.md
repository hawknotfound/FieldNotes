# Student Growth Roadmap

A 4-year framework for going from a first-year student with no experience to a graduating student with a portfolio, network, and clear direction.

**Who this is for**: Students who want to use their college years intentionally. Not just "get a degree" but actually grow.

## Year 1: Discovery and exploration

**Goal**: Try enough things to figure out what you enjoy and what you are good at.

### Semester 1 (Months 1-6)

- [ ] Attend 2-3 workshops or webinars (any topic that seems interesting)
- [ ] Join 1-2 student clubs or communities
- [ ] Set up LinkedIn and GitHub profiles
- [ ] Attend 1 online hackathon or competition (participate, do not worry about winning)
- [ ] Talk to 2 seniors about what they do outside the classroom
- [ ] Build 1 small project in any area that interests you

**By the end of Semester 1, you should have:**
- A basic LinkedIn profile
- Experience attending at least one event (online)
- A rough sense of 2-3 areas you want to explore further

### Semester 2 (Months 7-12)

- [ ] Choose 2 areas from your exploration to go deeper in
- [ ] Take a structured online course in one of those areas
- [ ] Attend 1 offline event (college fest, local meetup, hackathon)
- [ ] Build 1 project that is slightly more ambitious than your first
- [ ] Join a community (Discord/Telegram/WhatsApp) related to your interests
- [ ] Write 1 post on LinkedIn about something you learned

**By the end of Semester 2, you should have:**
- At least 2 projects on GitHub
- Experience attending an offline event
- Membership in 1-2 active communities
- A rough direction for Year 2

### Year 1 checkpoints
- [ ] Participated in 3+ events (mix of online and offline)
- [ ] Built 2+ projects
- [ ] Joined 2+ communities
- [ ] Set up online presence (LinkedIn, GitHub)
- [ ] Have a rough idea of what I enjoy

### Year 1 mistakes to avoid
- Trying to do everything (pick 2 areas, not 10)
- Comparing with seniors who have been doing this for years
- Waiting until you are "ready" to participate
- Building projects that nobody sees (learn to share work)

## Year 2: Building depth

**Goal**: Go deep in 1-2 areas. Build real competence.

### Semester 3 (Months 13-18)

- [ ] Choose 1 primary area to focus on for the year
- [ ] Complete a substantial project in this area (2-4 weeks of work)
- [ ] Participate in 2-3 events related to this area
- [ ] Write 1 article or thread explaining something you learned
- [ ] Help 1-2 juniors or classmates who are starting out
- [ ] Start contributing to open source or collaborative projects

### Semester 4 (Months 19-24)

- [ ] Lead a team in a hackathon or competition
- [ ] Participate in a national-level event (SIH, ICPC, national olympiad, etc.)
- [ ] Present at a college event or local meetup
- [ ] Build a project that solves a real problem (not just a tutorial project)
- [ ] Apply for 1 fellowship or selective program
- [ ] Review and update your portfolio

### Year 2 checkpoints
- [ ] Completed a substantial project in my chosen area
- [ ] Participated in national-level event
- [ ] Led a team at least once
- [ ] Presented or taught something publicly
- [ ] Contributed to a collaborative project

### Year 2 mistakes to avoid
- Staying in one area too long without trying adjacent things
- Building complex projects that never get finished
- Ignoring soft skills (communication, teamwork, presentation)
- Staying in your college bubble (connect with people outside)

## Year 3: Application and leadership

**Goal**: Apply what you have learned in real-world settings. Start leading.

### Semester 5 (Months 25-30)

- [ ] Intern or work on a real client/project (paid or unpaid, but real)
- [ ] Mentor 1-2 juniors who are where you were in Year 1
- [ ] Speak at a meetup or event (not just attend)
- [ ] Lead a college club or community initiative
- [ ] Apply for top-tier programs (Google Summer of Code, MLH Fellowship, research programs)
- [ ] Build a complex project that demonstrates depth

### Semester 6 (Months 31-36)

- [ ] Publish something significant (blog post, paper, open source library, case study)
- [ ] Compete in advanced-level competitions
- [ ] Build a professional network of 50+ people in your field
- [ ] Seek leadership roles (club president, event organizer, community lead)
- [ ] Identify 2-3 career paths you might pursue after graduation
- [ ] Speak with 5 professionals in your target field (informational interviews)

### Year 3 checkpoints
- [ ] Completed an internship or real-world project
- [ ] Mentored someone newer than me
- [ ] Spoke at an event or published something
- [ ] Held a leadership role
- [ ] Clear understanding of 2-3 post-graduation paths

### Year 3 mistakes to avoid
- Overcommitting to too many things (depth over breadth)
- Neglecting academics (grades still matter for many paths)
- Not applying for competitive programs (fear of rejection)
- Staying in the same community (diversify your network)

## Year 4: Transition to career

**Goal**: Use everything you have built to move into your next phase — job, grad school, startup, or fellowship.

### Semester 7 (Months 37-42)

- [ ] Finalize your post-graduation plan (job, higher studies, entrepreneurship)
- [ ] Prepare for placements or applications 6 months in advance
- [ ] Build a strong final portfolio showcasing your best work
- [ ] Write a detailed resume that reflects 3 years of growth
- [ ] Network specifically for your target path (recruiters, alumni, professors)
- [ ] Complete a capstone project or thesis (if applicable)

### Semester 8 (Months 43-48)

- [ ] Apply to jobs/grad schools/fellowships
- [ ] Go through interviews and selection processes
- [ ] Document your journey for juniors to learn from
- [ ] Leave your communities better than you found them
- [ ] Transition leadership of any clubs or initiatives you lead
- [ ] Plan your next 1-2 years post-graduation

### Year 4 checkpoints
- [ ] Clear post-graduation plan
- [ ] Resume and portfolio updated
- [ ] Applied to target opportunities
- [ ] Transitioned leadership roles
- [ ] Documented journey for others

### Year 4 mistakes to avoid
- Starting placement prep too late (start 6 months before)
- Forgetting to leverage your network
- Neglecting to document your journey
- Burning out in the final year

## The growth curve by year

| Year | Focus | Events attended | Projects built | Network size | Confidence level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | 3-5 | 2-3 | 10-20 | Low → Medium |
| 2 | Build depth | 5-8 | 3-5 | 20-50 | Medium |
| 3 | Lead | 5-8 | 2-3 (higher quality) | 50-100 | Medium → High |
| 4 | Transition | Varies | 1 (capstone/portfolio) | 100+ | High |

These are rough targets. Adjust based on your circumstances.

## How to measure progress

Not by comparison with others. By comparison with your past self.

**Each semester, ask:**
- What can I do now that I could not do 6 months ago?
- Who do I know now that I did not know 6 months ago?
- What have I completed that I did not have 6 months ago?

If you have answers to all three, you are growing.

## What to do if you start late

This roadmap assumes you start in Year 1. If you are in Year 2 or 3:

- **Year 2 starter**: Compress Year 1 into 1 semester. Focus on 1 area. Skip exploration.
- **Year 3 starter**: Do Years 1+2 in parallel. Focus on building projects and participating immediately.
- **Year 4 starter**: Focus on a single strong project and network aggressively for your target path.

It is never too late. The best time to start was 4 years ago. The second best time is now.
